The rubble trade, one link at a time

With almost no bulldozers working, Khan Younis’s debris is cleared by hand. Each link is a named person, with the day rate he gave the reporter.

Khan Younis · from the hammer to the tent

  1. Qassam al-Sane’e, 35

    Breaks the concrete

    Former web designer and repair-shop technician, displaced from Rafah; father of six

    $20a day, paid by a middleman

    We know it is harmful, we know it is dangerous, but we still work.

  2. Saleh Musbah, 33

    Moves the debris

    Construction worker before the war; about ten people live on his pay

    ₪50about $16.50 for a 12-hour day

    Fifty shekels, what can that do?

  3. Nahid Nabil Zo’rub, 33

    Runs a crew

    Ten years in demolition; leads about 15 men. A job that took 2–3 days now takes 3–4 weeks

    $16.50–30a day

    Now there are no bulldozers, no equipment, nothing.

  4. Tamir Breis, 35

    Buys and sorts

    Scrap dealer; straightens bent iron and cleans tiles for resale. Displaced nine times

    $16.50–20a day

    Before the war, life was much better than this.

  5. Families in tents

    Buys what can be used

    Tiles cover muddy floors, wood shores up shelters, iron becomes tent frames and furniture

    End of the linea chain made by destruction

Reported from the workplace

A harbour and a factory floor. The harbour story is from January; nothing on the boats has changed since.

“Today, I fish only to bring home lunch for my family”

The sea · Gaza

Before the war Abu Mohammed, 40, fished to sell his catch. Now he heads out in the early hours, stays within about 500 metres of the shore, and comes back with a meal; on some days with nothing.

Nizar Ayash, who heads Gaza’s fishermen’s union, told the reporter that about 90 percent of boats in Gaza City and the north were destroyed, and estimated the sector’s losses at $75 million.

If we sail, we fear being killed or arrested … And if we don’t, we fear our children will starve.

Abu Mohammed, to Shaimaa Eid for The Palestine Chronicle, 14 Jan 2026

Flour sacks become tote bags, and aid pallets become shelves

Sewing and carpentry · Gaza City

In a Gaza City factory run by the UN World Food Programme since April, about 113 women and 20 men work three-month shifts unpicking empty flour sacks into bags and turning pallets into furniture that is given away. The pay is 1,000 shekels a month.

Nisreen Abbas, 54, keeps 12 grandchildren and a husband who uses a wheelchair on it: “I bring food for my grandchildren; I bring medicine for my husband.”

I would like for this to be renewed, for us to keep working.

Warda Aliwa, 38, to CBC News, 2 Oct

Moving

  • Beit Furik, Nablus

    Single gate

    The town’s only way in and out. A 15-minute drive routinely took more than five hours, up to ten on the worst days.UN Special Rapporteur statement, 2 Oct

  • al-Maniya, south-east of Bethlehem

    Cut in two

    Earth mounds split the village into parts; the main entrance was already closed.WAFA, 8 Oct

  • Deir Ballut, Salfit

    Farm roads blocked

    Earth barriers on several roads west of the village in the middle of the olive harvest, keeping farmers off their land.WAFA, 8 Oct

  • Rafah crossing, Gaza–Egypt

    Open, limited

    185 people left and 150 entered in the week of 20–26 September, 86 of them medical patients.EUBAM Rafah, 20–26 Sep
